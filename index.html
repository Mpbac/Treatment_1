Here are some pro tips for how to get a good deal on your upcoming car purchase and loan. Feel free to bookmark this page or download a PDF of these tips.

Part I. Before Contacting a Dealer

  1. Research Your Target Vehicle
    1. Must haves: Good road test performance, average or better reliability, average or better owner satisfaction, good crash test performance. (Recommend: automatic emergency braking, blind spot warning, rear cross traffic warning.)
    2. Key features: What are the most important features to you (e.g., all-wheel drive, seating, safety rating, cargo space)?
    3. Narrow the options: Pick a vehicle type (compact, midsize sedan, SUV), choose a make and model (e.g., Ford F-150), select a trim that fits your needs (e.g., XLT or Platinum), and review trusted insights from Consumer Reports, Cars.com, or KBB.
    4. Get a ballpark of what you should be paying. Check a trusted source like TrueCar, Cars.com, or Edmunds for what people in your area are paying for your target vehicle.
    5. Your budget should factor in the purchase, fees, taxes, and insurance, which will likely be higher on a new car.
  2. Line up outside financing
    1. Get pre-approval for a loan (how to check) directly from a bank or credit union.
    2. Then insist that the dealer that offer to trim your loan cost and lower your monthly payment (see Step 5e).
  3. Know your trade-in's value
    1. If you plan to sell your old car, get at least two independent wholesale value estimates, such as from Consumer Reports or Kelley Blue Book (KBB).

Part II. Dealing with Dealers

  1. Lock down your deal before setting foot in a dealership!
    1. Before you go to a dealership, use email to get initial offers from multiple dealers and then negotiate better offers.
    2. See Step 5 for pro tips on getting and negotiating offers: The more you can get in writing before setting foot in a dealership, the better deal you'll get!
    3. Dealers may contact you after you check what you should be paying for your desired vehicle in Step 1c. Or it might be you making the first contact: You can find the Internet Sales Manager and/or Sales Reps on dealership websites. Either way...
  2. Use email to shop around smartly
    1. Email several dealers to request initial offers for the following separate pieces of your car-buying transaction, using our negotiation tools (sample scripts or AI assistant):
      1. Out-the-door (OTD) price: one all-inclusive figure that covers all taxes and fees, with no hidden costs or add-ons later. Do not include your trade-in (see next step)!
      2. A separate, all-in trade-in offer: Keep the trade-in separate from the OTD price and ask for the exact check amount they would write you today with no last-minute changes. Make sure the offer is valid for at least seven days so you have time to compare.
      3. Financing offer: a separate financing quote that details the interest rate (APR), loan term, and required down payment, tailored to your current credit score (how to look up your score).
    2. Negotiate by email! Their initial offer is rarely their final one....
      1. Negotiate each part of the deal separately! Dealers try to bundle together the car, financing, trade-in, and add-ons to keep you from negotiating effectively. See our (scripts) or just write:
        1. "We can talk about what you have to offer on financing after we finalize the price of the car." OR
        2. "We can talk about my potential trade-in later, after we finalize the price of the car."
      2. Focus on prices and rates, not just monthly payments. Dealers profit at your expense when they sell you on the monthly payment instead of the overall price.
        1. Try saying: "I'm not focused on the monthly payment-let's talk about the out-the-door price."
        2. Then, you can further lower your monthly payment by negotiating a longer term: "I have a loan pre-approval for 72 months at []% APR. Can you beat/match that?"
    3. Decline unnecessary add-ons: Dealers add overpriced add-ons (e.g., extended warranties, service contracts, coating, GAP insurance, etc.). Just say no to anything you haven't researched and decided you need!
    4. Trash junk fees. Many dealers add unnecessary fees-you should refuse to pay these. See our (Checklist) for what's legit and what's not.
    5. Leverage your outside offers. Be ready to walk away if the dealer doesn't meet or beat them. Try saying:
      1. "I have an out-the-door quote for $ from another dealer. Can you beat that?"
      2. I have an offer for % for months with no fees and no money down. Can you beat that?"
  3. Go to the dealership to complete the transaction!
    1. If the dealer tries to change the deal, show them what you have in writing.
    2. Repeat Step 5, as needed.
    3. Sign on the dotted lines to close your good deal!
    4. Enjoy your new vehicle!
